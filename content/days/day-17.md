# Introduction: From Problems to Solutions

In Day 16, we learned:

> Great products start with validated problems.

Now we need to answer:

> "What is the best way to solve this problem?"

Many teams make this mistake:

```text
Problem
  ↓
First Idea
  ↓
Development
```

A better approach:

```text
Problem
  ↓
Explore Multiple Solutions
  ↓
Evaluate Options
  ↓
Validate Best Ideas
  ↓
Build
```

# What Is Solution Discovery?

Solution discovery is the process of exploring, designing, and validating possible solutions for a customer problem.

It helps teams answer:

- What solutions could solve this problem?
- Which solution creates the most value?
- Which solution is technically feasible?
- Which solution should we test first?

---

# Problem First, Solution Second

A common Product Management principle:

> Fall in love with the problem, not the solution.

Example:

Customer says:

> "Add an export button."

A weak PM response:

> "Let's build export."

A strong PM response:

> "Why do users need export?"

Possible underlying problems:

- Users need to share information.
- Users need backup data.
- Users need analysis outside the product.

Different problems require different solutions.

---

# Feature Request vs Customer Need

Feature request:

> "I want dark mode."

Customer need:

> "I want to use the app comfortably at night."

Possible solutions:

- Dark mode
- Better brightness settings
- Automatic night mode
- Reduced visual complexity

The requested feature is only one possible solution.

---

# The Solution Discovery Process

A practical process:

```text
Understand Problem
        ↓
Generate Ideas
        ↓
Explore Alternatives
        ↓
Evaluate Solutions
        ↓
Create Hypotheses
        ↓
Test Solutions
```

---

# Step 1: Understand the Problem Context

Before brainstorming solutions, review:

- Who has the problem?
- When does it happen?
- Why does it matter?
- What alternatives exist today?

Example:

Problem:

> Beginner investors do not feel confident making their first trade.

Context:

- New users
- High uncertainty
- Fear of losing money

Now we can explore solutions.

---

# Step 2: Generate Multiple Solutions

The goal is quantity first.

Do not judge ideas too early.

A good brainstorming session creates many possibilities.

Example problem:

> Users don't know which stock to buy.

Possible ideas:

1. Educational content
2. Stock recommendations
3. Expert opinions
4. Investment simulator
5. Portfolio templates
6. AI investment assistant
7. Community discussions

---

# Ideation Techniques

## 1. Brainstorming

The most common technique.

Rules:

- Generate many ideas.
- Avoid criticism during generation.
- Build on others' ideas.
- Focus on the problem.

---

## 2. Crazy 8s

A fast design exercise.

Process:

- Take a paper.
- Divide it into 8 sections.
- Create 8 solution ideas in 8 minutes.

Purpose:

Force creative thinking.

---

## 3. Reverse Thinking

Ask:

> "How could we make this problem worse?"

Example:

Problem:

Users struggle with onboarding.

Reverse ideas:

- Add more steps.
- Hide important information.
- Use complicated language.

Then reverse them:

- Reduce steps.
- Show clear information.
- Use simple language.

---

## 4. Analogies

Look at how other industries solve similar problems.

Example:

Trading app onboarding:

Borrow ideas from:

- Gaming tutorials
- Language learning apps
- Financial education platforms

---

# Step 3: Evaluate Solutions

Not every idea is valuable.

Evaluate solutions based on:

---

# Customer Value

Does it solve an important customer problem?

Example:

A beautiful animation may not solve a painful problem.

---

# Business Value

Does it support business goals?

Examples:

- Revenue growth
- Retention
- Acquisition
- Cost reduction

---

# Feasibility

Can we realistically build it?

Consider:

- Engineering effort
- Technology limitations
- Resources

---

# Usability

Will customers understand and use it?

---

# Strategic Fit

Does it align with the product vision?

---

# Solution Evaluation Matrix

A simple framework:

| Solution | Customer Value | Business Value | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| AI assistant | High | High | High |
| Tutorial | Medium | Medium | Low |
| Community forum | Medium | Low | Medium |

A good PM balances impact and effort.

---

# Impact vs Effort Matrix

A common prioritization tool:

```text
             HIGH IMPACT

                 |
                 |
  Quick Wins     |     Big Bets
                 |
-----------------|----------------
                 |
  Avoid          |     Low Priority
                 |
             LOW IMPACT
```

---

# Quick Wins

High impact, low effort.

Example:

Improve confusing onboarding text.

---

# Big Bets

High impact, high effort.

Example:

Build an AI-powered financial advisor.

---

# Low Priority

Low impact, high effort.

Avoid unless strategically important.

---

# Step 4: Create Solution Hypotheses

A solution hypothesis is a statement about what you believe will happen.

Template:

> We believe that [solution] will help [user] achieve [outcome]. We will know this is true when [measurement].

Example:

> We believe that a guided investment onboarding flow will help beginner investors complete their first trade. We will know this is true when first-trade completion increases by 20%.

---

# Good Solution Hypotheses Include

## User

Who benefits?

---

## Solution

What are we changing?

---

## Expected Outcome

What improvement do we expect?

---

## Measurement

How will we know?

---

# Example: LinkedIn

Problem:

Job seekers struggle to find relevant jobs.

---

## Possible Solutions

Solution 1:

Improve job recommendations.

Solution 2:

Career coaching assistant.

Solution 3:

Profile optimization tool.

---

## Hypothesis

> We believe that AI profile recommendations will help job seekers improve their profiles, increasing recruiter engagement.

Measurement:

- Profile completion rate
- Recruiter views
- Job applications

# Example: Stock Trading Platform

Problem:

New investors are afraid to make their first investment.

---

## Possible Solutions

### Solution A

Investment education.

### Solution B

Practice trading simulator.

### Solution C

Guided first investment.

---

## Evaluation

Education:

- Value: High
- Effort: Low

Simulator:

- Value: High
- Effort: Medium

Guided investment:

- Value: Very High
- Effort: High

Possible decision:

Start with education + guided onboarding.

# Solution Validation

Before building a complete solution, test it.

Methods:

---

## Prototype Testing

Create a simple design.

Ask users:

- Does this solve your problem?
- Would you use it?
- What is confusing?

---

## Fake Door Test

Measure interest before building.

Example:

Add:

> "Create AI investment plan"

Measure clicks.

---

## Concierge MVP

Manually provide the experience.

Example:

Before building AI recommendations:

A human expert creates recommendations manually.

Learn first.

Automate later.

---

# The Difference Between Idea and Validated Solution

Idea:

> "Users might like this."

Validated solution:

> "Research shows users need this, understand it, and are willing to use it."

---

# Common Solution Discovery Mistakes

## Mistake #1

Building the first idea.

The first idea is rarely the best.

---

## Mistake #2

Ignoring alternatives.

Many solutions can solve one problem.

---

## Mistake #3

Focusing only on features.

The goal is customer outcomes.

---

## Mistake #4

Building before validating.

Test assumptions early.

---

## Mistake #5

Ignoring business constraints.

A solution must work for both customers and the company.

---

# Interview Question

## Question

How do you choose between multiple possible solutions?

## Strong Answer

I evaluate solutions based on customer value, business impact, feasibility, effort, and strategic alignment. I avoid choosing the first idea and instead compare multiple approaches, create hypotheses, and validate the strongest options before committing resources.

# Mini Case Study

You are the PM of a banking app.

Problem:

> Users forget to pay bills on time.

Generate solutions.

Possible ideas:

1. Reminder notifications
2. Automatic payments
3. Calendar integration
4. Smart financial assistant
5. Payment scheduling

Evaluate each solution using:

- Customer value
- Business value
- Effort
- Risk

Choose one solution to test first.

---

# Key Takeaways

1. Start with problems, not features.
2. Generate multiple solutions before deciding.
3. Evaluate ideas based on value and effort.
4. Create solution hypotheses.
5. Validate solutions before investing heavily.
6. The best solution is not always the biggest one.
7. Product Managers optimize customer outcomes, not feature output.

# Introduction: Measuring Product Success

A Product Manager's job is not:

> "Build features."

A Product Manager's job is:

> "Create measurable customer and business value."

After launching a feature, we need to understand:

- Did users adopt it?
- Did it solve their problem?
- Did it improve business outcomes?
- Should we invest more?

Metrics help answer these questions.

---

# What Are Product Metrics?

Product metrics are measurable data points that show:

- How users interact with a product
- Whether the product creates value
- Whether business goals are being achieved

Examples:

- Number of active users
- Conversion rate
- Retention rate
- Revenue per user
- Feature adoption

---

# Metrics vs KPIs

These terms are often confused.

---

# Metric

Any measurable value.

Example:

Number of app downloads.

---

# KPI (Key Performance Indicator)

A metric directly connected to an important business goal.

Example:

Monthly active users for a social network.

---

Every KPI is a metric, but not every metric is a KPI.

---

# Why Product Metrics Matter

## 1. Make Better Decisions

Without metrics:

> "I think users like this feature."

With metrics:

> "Feature usage increased activation by 15%."

---

## 2. Understand Customers

Metrics reveal:

- What users do
- Where they struggle
- What creates value

---

## 3. Prioritize Work

Metrics help decide:

- What to improve
- What to stop
- Where to invest

---

## 4. Measure Business Impact

Products exist to create value for both:

- Customers
- Businesses

---

# Product Analytics Funnel

Many digital products follow a funnel:

```text
Acquisition
     ↓
Activation
     ↓
Engagement
     ↓
Retention
     ↓
Revenue
     ↓
Referral
```

This is often called the:

# AARRR Framework

Created by Dave McClure.

Also known as:

> Pirate Metrics

---

# 1. Acquisition

## Question:

How do users discover your product?

Examples:

- Website visits
- App downloads
- Marketing campaigns
- Search traffic

---

## Metrics

### Website Visitors

Number of people visiting your website.

---

### Cost Per Acquisition (CPA)

How much does it cost to acquire one user?

Formula:

```
CPA = Marketing Cost / New Customers
```

---

### Signup Rate

Percentage of visitors who create accounts.

---

# Example: LinkedIn

Acquisition channels:

- Search engines
- Referrals
- Professional communities

Metric:

Number of new professional accounts created.

---

# 2. Activation

## Question:

Do users experience the first value of the product?

Activation is often called:

> The "Aha Moment"

---

Examples:

LinkedIn:

Creating a complete profile.

---

Spotify:

Listening to the first song.

---

Trading Platform:

Completing the first investment.

---

## Activation Metrics

Examples:

- Account completion rate
- First successful action
- Time to first value

---

# Example:

A trading app has:

100,000 new users.

20,000 users make their first trade.

Activation rate:

```
20%
```

---

# 3. Engagement

## Question:

How frequently do users interact with the product?

---

Metrics:

## Daily Active Users (DAU)

Number of users active each day.

---

## Monthly Active Users (MAU)

Number of users active each month.

---

## DAU / MAU Ratio

Measures product stickiness.

Example:

DAU = 100,000

MAU = 500,000

Ratio:

20%

---

# Example:

Social products usually care deeply about engagement.

LinkedIn measures:

- Posts created
- Connections made
- Content views

---

# 4. Retention

## Question:

Do users continue using the product?

A product can acquire many users but fail if users leave.

---

## Retention Rate

Formula:

```
Users who return later
----------------------
Original users
```

---

Example:

1000 users sign up.

300 return after 30 days.

30-day retention:

30%

---

# Why Retention Matters

Retention indicates:

- Product value
- Customer satisfaction
- Long-term health

---

# 5. Revenue

## Question:

Does the product create business value?

---

Metrics:

## Revenue

Total money generated.

---

## Average Revenue Per User (ARPU)

Formula:

```
Revenue / Number of Users
```

---

## Lifetime Value (LTV)

Estimated total value from one customer.

---

## Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)

Cost to acquire a customer.

---

# LTV vs CAC

A healthy business usually wants:

```
LTV > CAC
```

Example:

Customer lifetime value:

$300

Acquisition cost:

$100

Healthy relationship.

---

# 6. Referral

## Question:

Do users recommend the product?

Happy customers often bring new customers.

---

Metrics:

## Net Promoter Score (NPS)

Question:

> "How likely are you to recommend this product?"

Scale:

0–10

---

## Referral Rate

Percentage of users who invite others.

---

# North Star Metric

One of the most important concepts in Product Management.

A North Star Metric represents the core value users receive from your product.

It answers:

> "What single measurement best represents product success?"

---

# Examples of North Star Metrics

## LinkedIn

Possible North Star:

> Successful professional connections created.

---

## Spotify

Possible North Star:

> Hours of meaningful listening.

---

## Airbnb

Possible North Star:

> Successful stays completed.

---

## Trading Platform

Possible North Star:

> Successful investment journeys completed.

---

# Why North Star Metrics Matter

They:

- Align teams
- Prevent random feature development
- Focus everyone on customer value

---

# Vanity Metrics vs Actionable Metrics

A common PM mistake:

Tracking numbers that look impressive but do not help decisions.

---

# Vanity Metrics

Examples:

- Total downloads
- Total registered users
- Page views

These may look good but can be misleading.

---

# Actionable Metrics

Help teams make decisions.

Examples:

- Activation rate
- Retention rate
- Conversion rate
- Feature adoption

---

# Example

Bad metric:

> We have 5 million downloads.

Question:

How many people actually use the app?

Better metric:

> Monthly active users increased 20%.

---

# Input Metrics vs Output Metrics

Another important distinction.

---

# Output Metrics

The final result.

Examples:

- Revenue
- Retention
- Growth

---

# Input Metrics

Actions that influence outputs.

Examples:

For retention:

Input:

- Number of users completing onboarding
- Number of users receiving notifications

Output:

- Monthly retention

---

# Example:

Goal:

Increase first-time investors.

Output:

First-trade completion.

Inputs:

- Educational content completion
- Identity verification success
- Investment recommendations viewed

---

# Product Metrics Example: Stock Trading Platform

Goal:

Increase active investors.

---

## Acquisition Metrics

- New registrations
- Marketing conversion rate

---

## Activation Metrics

- Verification completion
- First deposit
- First trade

---

## Engagement Metrics

- Weekly portfolio views
- Number of trades

---

## Retention Metrics

- Monthly active investors
- Repeat investments

---

## Revenue Metrics

- Trading fees
- Assets under management

# Creating a Product Dashboard

A good dashboard should answer:

## Business Question

What are we trying to improve?

Example:

Increase customer retention.

---

## Key Metrics

Choose meaningful measurements.

Example:

- Monthly active users
- Retention rate
- Repeat transactions

---

## Segmentation

Look at different user groups.

Example:

Compare:

- New users
- Experienced users

---

## Trends

Look at changes over time.

One number is not enough.

---

# Common Product Metrics Mistakes

## Mistake #1

Tracking too many metrics.

More data does not always mean better decisions.

---

## Mistake #2

Using metrics without context.

A number needs interpretation.

---

## Mistake #3

Optimizing the wrong metric.

Example:

Increasing clicks but reducing customer satisfaction.

---

## Mistake #4

Ignoring qualitative feedback.

Numbers explain what happened.

Research explains why.

---

## Mistake #5

Not connecting metrics to goals.

Every metric should support a decision.

---

# Interview Question

## Question

What metrics would you track for a new product?

## Strong Answer

I would first define the product goal and identify the key user value moment. Then I would track metrics across the funnel, including acquisition, activation, engagement, retention, and revenue. I would focus on actionable metrics that help us understand user behavior and business impact rather than vanity metrics.

---

# Key Takeaways

1. Metrics help PMs make better decisions.
2. Good metrics connect customer value with business value.
3. North Star Metrics align teams around impact.
4. Activation and retention are often more important than acquisition.
5. Avoid vanity metrics.
6. Data shows what happened; research explains why.
7. Great PMs measure outcomes, not output.

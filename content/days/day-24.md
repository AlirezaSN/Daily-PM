# Introduction: Why Vision Matters

Every successful product starts with a clear understanding of:

> "What are we trying to achieve?"

Without a clear vision:

```text
Customer Request
       ↓
Feature Idea
       ↓
Development
       ↓
Another Feature
       ↓
No Clear Direction
```

With a clear vision:

```text
Vision
  ↓
Strategy
  ↓
Goals
  ↓
Roadmap
  ↓
Features
  ↓
Customer Value
```

Vision creates alignment.

---

# What Is Product Vision?

Product vision describes the future state a product wants to create.

Definition:

> A product vision is a long-term statement describing the impact a product aims to create for customers and the market.

A vision answers:

- What future do we want?
- Who are we helping?
- What change are we creating?

---

Meaning:

LinkedIn is not only a job website.

---

## Airbnb

# Vision vs Mission

These terms are often confused.

---

# Product Vision

Focus:

The future.

Question:

> Where do we want to go?

Example:

"Make investing accessible for everyone."

---

# Product Mission

Focus:

The current purpose and actions.

Question:

> What do we do every day to achieve our vision?

Example:

"Provide simple investment tools and education for beginner investors."

---

# Difference Example

Company:

Stock Trading Platform

## Vision

> A world where everyone can confidently build wealth.

---

## Mission

> Provide simple, transparent, and educational investing experiences.

---

# Vision vs Strategy vs Roadmap

Let's connect all concepts.

```text
VISION
The future we want to create

        ↓

MISSION
Why we exist and what we do

        ↓

STRATEGY
How we will win

        ↓

ROADMAP
What we will build

        ↓

FEATURES
Specific solutions
```

---

# Why Product Vision Matters

## 1. Creates Long-Term Direction

Products evolve over years.

Vision keeps teams focused.

Example:

A company may build:

- Mobile app
- Website
- AI assistant
- Community features

But all should support the same vision.

---

## 2. Aligns Teams

Different teams need the same understanding.

Without vision:

Engineering:

> Build requested features.

Marketing:

> Promote products.

Sales:

> Sell anything possible.

---

With vision:

Everyone works toward the same outcome.

---

## 3. Helps Prioritization

A PM receives hundreds of ideas.

Vision helps answer:

> Does this move us closer to our goal?

---

Example:

Vision:

"Help beginners invest confidently."

Feature request:

"Add advanced derivatives trading."

Question:

Does this support beginner confidence?

Maybe not.

---

## 4. Inspires Customers and Employees

A strong vision creates emotional connection.

People want to work on meaningful problems.

---

# Characteristics of a Strong Product Vision

A good vision should be:

---

# 1. Customer-Centered

Focus on customer impact.

Bad:

> Become the biggest technology company.

Better:

> Help people communicate more easily.

---

# 2. Long-Term

Vision should last years.

Bad:

> Increase app downloads by 20%.

That is a goal.

---

Better:

> Make financial services accessible worldwide.

---

# 3. Inspirational

It should create motivation.

---

# 4. Clear and Simple

Everyone should understand it.

Avoid:

- Complex language
- Technical terms
- Business jargon

---

# 5. Ambitious but Realistic

A vision should challenge the organization.

Too small:

> Improve our login page.

Too unrealistic:

> Solve all financial problems.

---

# Creating a Product Vision

A useful framework:

## Step 1: Understand the Customer Problem

Ask:

- What problem exists?
- Why does it matter?
- Who experiences it?

---

Example:

Problem:

Many beginners avoid investing because it feels complicated.

---

## Step 2: Define Desired Future

Ask:

> What should the world look like if we succeed?

Example:

Everyone can confidently invest.

---

## Step 3: Identify Your Role

Ask:

> How will our product help create this future?

Example:

By simplifying investing education and execution.

---

## Step 4: Write the Vision Statement

Template:

> We want to create a future where [customer] can [achieve outcome].

Example:

> We want to create a future where everyone can confidently manage their financial future.

---

# Vision Statement Frameworks

## Framework 1: For [Customer]

Template:

> For [target customer], our product helps [problem] by [solution].

Example:

> For beginner investors, our platform helps build wealth by making investing simple and educational.

---

## Framework 2: Future State

Template:

> A world where [desired future].

Example:

> A world where everyone can access financial opportunities.

---

## Framework 3: Transformation

Template:

> Transform [current situation] into [future state].

Example:

> Transform investing from a complex activity into an accessible experience.

# Connecting Vision to Daily Decisions

A common mistake:

Companies write a vision document and forget it.

A vision should influence:

---

## Feature Prioritization

Question:

Does this feature support the vision?

---

## Product Metrics

Question:

Are we moving closer to the desired future?

---

## Hiring

Question:

Are we building the right team?

---

## Partnerships

Question:

Does this partnership support our direction?

---

# Common Vision Mistakes

## Mistake #1

Making vision about the company, not customers.

Bad:

> Become the largest company.

Better:

> Help customers achieve something meaningful.

---

## Mistake #2

Confusing vision with goals.

Bad:

> Increase revenue by 30%.

That is a business goal.

---

## Mistake #3

Making it too generic.

Bad:

> Make the world better.

Too broad.

---

## Mistake #4

Creating a vision nobody remembers.

If employees cannot repeat it, it is not useful.

---

## Mistake #5

Ignoring changes in the market.

Vision can be stable, but strategy must adapt.

---

# Interview Question

## Question

What makes a good product vision?

## Strong Answer

A good product vision is customer-centered, long-term, inspiring, and clear. It describes the future impact the product wants to create and helps teams make consistent strategic decisions. It should guide strategy, prioritization, and product development.

# Key Takeaways

1. Vision defines the future a product wants to create.
2. Mission explains why the product exists.
3. Strategy explains how to achieve the vision.
4. Roadmaps translate strategy into action.
5. A strong vision is customer-focused.
6. Vision helps teams prioritize better.
7. Great PMs connect daily decisions to long-term impact.

# Why Customer Journey Mapping Matters

A common mistake in product development is focusing only on individual features.

Example:

A banking team thinks:

> "We need a better transfer button."

But customers do not think in terms of buttons.

Customers think in terms of goals:

> "I need to send money quickly and confidently."

The customer's experience includes many steps before and after using a feature.

Customer Journey Mapping helps Product Managers understand the complete experience.

---

# What Is a Customer Journey?

A customer journey is the complete experience a customer goes through while trying to achieve a goal.

It includes:

- Actions
- Thoughts
- Feelings
- Problems
- Opportunities

Example:

A LinkedIn user journey:

```
Need a new job
      ↓
Search for opportunities
      ↓
Review job posts
      ↓
Apply
      ↓
Interview
      ↓
Get hired
```

The product experience exists across the entire journey.

---

# What Is Customer Journey Mapping?

Customer Journey Mapping is a visual representation of the steps customers take while interacting with a product or service.

It helps teams understand:

- What customers do
- What customers think
- What customers feel
- Where customers struggle
- Where the product can improve

---

# Why PMs Need Journey Maps

Journey maps help Product Managers:

## 1. Understand Customer Reality

Teams often understand their own product better than their customers.

A journey map forces the team to see the experience from the customer's perspective.

---

## 2. Find Pain Points

Customers may struggle at points the team never considered.

Example:

A trading app may work perfectly after login.

But users may struggle with:

- Account verification
- Adding money
- Understanding risk
- Placing the first order

---

## 3. Identify Opportunities

Every frustration is a potential product opportunity.

Example:

Pain:

> Users don't know which investment to choose.

Opportunity:

> Add educational guidance and recommendations.

---

## 4. Align Teams

Marketing, Product, Design, Engineering, and Support may see different parts of the experience.

A journey map creates a shared understanding.

---

# Customer Journey vs User Flow

These concepts are related but different.

---

# User Flow

Focuses on steps inside a product.

Example:

```
Open App
 ↓
Login
 ↓
Search
 ↓
Purchase
 ↓
Confirmation
```

It describes interactions with the interface.

---

# Customer Journey

Includes the broader experience.

Example:

```
Need a product
 ↓
Research options
 ↓
Compare alternatives
 ↓
Visit website
 ↓
Create account
 ↓
Purchase
 ↓
Receive product
 ↓
Get support
```

It includes emotions, expectations, and external experiences.

---

# Components of a Customer Journey Map

A typical journey map contains:

1. Customer Persona
2. Customer Goal
3. Journey Stages
4. Customer Actions
5. Customer Thoughts
6. Customer Emotions
7. Pain Points
8. Opportunities

Let's explore each.

---

# 1. Customer Persona

The journey should represent a specific customer.

Example:

Persona:

> Beginner investor using a mobile trading app.

Not:

> All users.

Different personas have different journeys.

---

# 2. Customer Goal

What is the customer trying to accomplish?

Examples:

LinkedIn:

> Find a better job.

Uber:

> Reach a destination quickly.

Trading app:

> Buy a stock confidently.

---

# 3. Journey Stages

Break the experience into major phases.

A common framework:

```
Awareness
    ↓
Consideration
    ↓
Decision
    ↓
Usage
    ↓
Retention
    ↓
Advocacy
```

Different products may have different stages.

---

# 4. Customer Actions

What does the customer do?

Example:

Trading app:

Stage: First investment

Actions:

- Search for stock
- Review information
- Enter order
- Confirm purchase

---

# 5. Customer Thoughts

What questions are in the customer's mind?

Examples:

Before signup:

> "Is this platform trustworthy?"

Before buying:

> "Am I making the right decision?"

---

# 6. Customer Emotions

How does the customer feel?

Common emotions:

- Excited
- Confused
- Worried
- Frustrated
- Confident

Emotion is a powerful indicator of experience quality.

---

# 7. Pain Points

Where does the customer struggle?

Examples:

- Too many steps
- Missing information
- Slow process
- Lack of trust
- Confusing interface

---

# 8. Opportunities

How can the product improve the journey?

Example:

Pain:

> Users don't understand investment risks.

Opportunity:

> Add educational explanations before purchase.

---

# Example: LinkedIn Job Search Journey Map

## Persona

Sarah:

A software engineer looking for a new job.

---

## Goal

Find and apply for relevant jobs.

---

## Stage 1: Discover

### Actions

- Opens LinkedIn.
- Searches jobs.

### Thoughts

> "Are there good opportunities here?"

### Emotions

Curious.

### Pain Points

Too many irrelevant jobs.

### Opportunities

Improve job recommendations.

---

## Stage 2: Evaluate

### Actions

- Reviews job descriptions.
- Checks company information.

### Thoughts

> "Is this company a good fit?"

### Emotions

Interested but uncertain.

### Pain Points

Missing salary information.

### Opportunities

Add salary transparency.

---

## Stage 3: Apply

### Actions

- Upload resume.
- Complete application.

### Thoughts

> "This is taking too long."

### Emotions

Frustrated.

### Pain Points

Long application process.

### Opportunities

Improve Easy Apply.

---

# Example: Stock Trading Platform Journey

## Persona

Ali:

Beginner investor.

---

## Goal

Make the first investment safely.

---

## Stage 1: Registration

Actions:

- Download app.
- Create account.

Thoughts:

> "Can I trust this platform?"

Emotion:

Uncertain.

Pain:

Complex verification.

Opportunity:

Simplify onboarding.

---

## Stage 2: Learning

Actions:

- Searches stocks.
- Reads information.

Thoughts:

> "Which stock should I choose?"

Emotion:

Confused.

Pain:

Too much financial information.

Opportunity:

Provide simplified explanations.

---

## Stage 3: First Trade

Actions:

- Places order.

Thoughts:

> "What if I lose money?"

Emotion:

Anxious.

Pain:

Lack of confidence.

Opportunity:

Add risk guidance.

---

# Moments That Matter

Not every step in a journey has equal importance.

Some moments strongly influence customer perception.

These are called:

> Moments That Matter

Examples:

## Banking App

First successful transaction.

---

## E-commerce

First delivery experience.

---

## LinkedIn

First successful job connection.

---

## Trading Platform

First completed trade.

Improving these moments often creates significant impact.

---

# How to Create a Customer Journey Map

Follow these steps:

---

## Step 1: Choose a Persona

Do not map everyone.

Choose one customer type.

---

## Step 2: Define the Goal

What is the customer trying to achieve?

---

## Step 3: Identify Journey Stages

Break the experience into meaningful phases.

---

## Step 4: Research Customer Behavior

Use:

- Interviews
- Analytics
- Surveys
- Support tickets

---

## Step 5: Document Actions, Thoughts, and Feelings

Capture the customer's perspective.

---

## Step 6: Identify Pain Points

Where does the experience fail?

---

## Step 7: Find Opportunities

Prioritize improvements.

---

# Journey Maps and Product Strategy

Journey maps help answer strategic questions:

- Where are customers struggling?
- Which problems are most important?
- Where should we invest?
- Which improvements create the most value?

A roadmap should not only contain features.

It should contain improvements to important customer journeys.

---

# Common Mistakes

## Mistake #1

Creating journey maps from assumptions.

Use research.

---

## Mistake #2

Mapping internal processes instead of customer experiences.

Focus on customers.

---

## Mistake #3

Trying to map every customer.

Start with important personas.

---

## Mistake #4

Creating maps and never using them.

Journey maps should influence decisions.

---

## Mistake #5

Focusing only on problems.

Also identify moments of delight.

---

# Interview Question

## Question

What is the value of customer journey mapping?

## Strong Answer

Customer journey mapping helps Product Managers understand the complete customer experience, identify pain points, discover opportunities, and align teams around customer needs. It shifts the focus from individual features to the customer's overall goal and experience.

# Key Takeaways

1. Customers experience journeys, not features.
2. Journey maps reveal hidden problems.
3. Good journey maps are based on research.
4. Different personas have different journeys.
5. Emotional experience matters.
6. Moments that matter deserve special attention.
7. Journey maps help teams prioritize better solutions.

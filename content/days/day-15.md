# Introduction: Building the Right Product

A common product failure is:

> Building something correctly that nobody needs.

Many teams are excellent at delivery:

- Writing code
- Designing interfaces
- Releasing features

But they fail because they did not answer the most important question:

> "Are we solving the right problem?"

Product discovery exists to answer this question.

---

# What Is Product Discovery?

Product discovery is the process of understanding:

- Which customer problems are worth solving
- Which opportunities create value
- Which solutions should be built
- Whether assumptions are correct

The goal is to reduce uncertainty before investing significant resources.

---

# Discovery vs Delivery

Product teams usually have two major activities:

```text
Discovery
    ↓
Understand problems
    ↓
Generate solutions
    ↓
Validate assumptions

Delivery
    ↓
Build solution
    ↓
Launch product
    ↓
Measure results
```

Both are necessary.

---

# Product Discovery

Discovery answers:

- Who is the customer?
- What problem do they have?
- How important is the problem?
- Why does it happen?
- What solutions might work?
- Will customers use it?

---

# Product Delivery

Delivery answers:

- How do we build it?
- How long will it take?
- Who will implement it?
- How do we launch it?
- How do we maintain it?

---

# The Product Discovery Mindset

Traditional mindset:

> "The CEO requested a feature. Let's build it."

Discovery mindset:

> "What customer problem created this request?"

---

Example:

Customer request:

> "Add dark mode."

Feature thinking:

Build dark mode.

Discovery thinking:

Ask:

- Why do users want dark mode?
- Is the problem eye strain?
- Is it battery usage?
- Is it night usage?

The solution may be different from the original request.

---

# Why Product Discovery Matters

## 1. Reduces Waste

Building features is expensive.

Discovery helps avoid:

- Unused features
- Wrong assumptions
- Poor investments

---

## 2. Improves Product Decisions

Instead of:

> "I think users want this."

You can say:

> "Research shows this problem affects 60% of active users."

---

## 3. Creates Better Customer Outcomes

Discovery keeps teams focused on customer value.

---

# The Product Discovery Process

A simplified discovery process:

```text
Identify Opportunity
          ↓
Understand Problem
          ↓
Generate Solutions
          ↓
Validate Solution
          ↓
Decide What to Build
```

Let's explore each step.

---

# Step 1: Identify Opportunities

An opportunity is a potential area where the product can create more value.

Sources of opportunities:

- Customer feedback
- User research
- Analytics
- Market trends
- Business goals
- Competitive analysis

---

Example:

Analytics:

> 40% of users abandon onboarding.

Opportunity:

> Improve activation experience.

---

# Step 2: Understand the Problem

Before solving anything, understand the problem deeply.

Questions:

- Who experiences this problem?
- How often does it happen?
- How painful is it?
- What alternatives exist?
- Why does it matter?

---

# Problem Statement

A good problem statement describes:

- User
- Situation
- Problem
- Impact

Example:

Bad:

> "We need better onboarding."

Good:

> "New investors struggle to complete their first trade because they do not understand market terminology, causing low activation rates."

---

# Step 3: Prioritize Problems

Not every problem deserves attention.

Evaluate problems based on:

## Customer Impact

How many users experience it?

---

## Problem Severity

How painful is it?

---

## Business Value

Does solving it support company goals?

---

## Strategic Fit

Does it align with product direction?

---

A simple framework:

```text
Opportunity Score =

Customer Impact × Business Value × Strategic Fit
```

---

# Step 4: Generate Solutions

Once the problem is clear, explore possible solutions.

Avoid jumping to the first idea.

Example:

Problem:

> Users don't trust investment decisions.

Possible solutions:

- Educational content
- Risk indicators
- Expert recommendations
- Portfolio simulations
- Community insights

Multiple solutions may solve the same problem.

---

# Step 5: Validate Solutions

Before building, test assumptions.

Questions:

- Will users want this?
- Can they understand it?
- Will they use it?
- Does it create value?

---

# Product Discovery Experiments

Experiments help teams learn quickly.

---

# 1. Customer Interviews

Purpose:

Understand problems and motivations.

Example:

Interview investors about decision-making challenges.

---

# 2. Surveys

Purpose:

Measure how common a problem is.

Example:

Ask 5,000 users about investment confidence.

---

# 3. Prototype Testing

Purpose:

Test designs before development.

Example:

Show a clickable trading flow.

---

# 4. Landing Page Tests

Purpose:

Measure interest.

Example:

Create a page describing a new feature and measure signups.

---

# 5. Fake Door Tests

Purpose:

Measure demand before building.

Example:

Add a button:

> "Enable Advanced Analytics"

If users click:

Show:

> "Coming soon."

Measure interest.

---

# 6. A/B Testing

Purpose:

Compare alternatives.

Example:

Compare two onboarding experiences.

---

```text

   ↓
Define
   ↓

   ↓
```

Explore users and problems.

---

## Define

---

## Develop

---

## Deliver

Created by Teresa Torres.

Structure:

```text

        ↓

        ↓

        ↓

```

Business goal:

Increase first-time investors.

- They don't understand investing.

- They don't know what to buy.

# Common Discovery Mistakes

## Mistake #1

Starting with solutions.

Bad:

> "Let's build an AI chatbot."

Better:

> "What customer problem would an AI chatbot solve?"

---

## Mistake #2

Assuming customer requests are solutions.

Customers describe problems through feature requests.

---

## Mistake #3

Skipping validation.

Building without testing assumptions creates risk.

---

## Mistake #4

Only listening to the loudest customers.

One customer's opinion is not always representative.

---

## Mistake #5

Confusing discovery with endless research.

Discovery should lead to decisions.

---

# Interview Question

## Question

What is the difference between product discovery and product delivery?

## Strong Answer

Product discovery focuses on identifying valuable customer problems and validating possible solutions before building. Product delivery focuses on implementing, launching, and improving the chosen solution. Discovery helps teams build the right product, while delivery helps them build the product correctly.

---

# Mini Case Study

You are the PM of a food delivery app.

The CEO says:

> "We need to add a loyalty program."

Before building it, apply discovery.

Answer:

1. What problem might this solve?
2. Who experiences this problem?
3. How would you validate the problem?
4. What alternative solutions could solve it?
5. What experiment would you run first?

---

# Key Takeaways

1. Product discovery helps teams build the right products.
2. Discovery happens before major investment.
3. Start with problems, not solutions.
4. Validate assumptions through experiments.
5. Good PMs reduce uncertainty.
6. Customer insights should drive product decisions.
7. Discovery and delivery are equally important.

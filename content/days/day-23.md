# Introduction: From Building Features to Building Direction

Many Product Managers start their careers by focusing on execution:

- Writing requirements
- Managing backlog
- Coordinating teams
- Launching features

These are important skills.

However, senior Product Managers must answer bigger questions:

- Which customers should we focus on?
- Which problems matter most?
- Where should we invest resources?
- How will we differentiate?
- What does success look like?

This is product strategy.

---

# What Is Product Strategy?

Product strategy is a plan that explains:

> How a product will achieve business goals by solving meaningful customer problems.

A good product strategy connects:

```text
Customer Needs
        +
Business Goals
        +
Market Opportunity
        +
Product Capabilities

        ↓

Product Direction
```

---

# Product Strategy Answers Three Questions

## 1. Where Are We Going?

The product direction.

Example:

> Become the easiest investment platform for beginners.

---

## 2. Why Are We Going There?

The reason.

Example:

> Millions of new investors struggle with complex financial products.

---

## 3. How Will We Win?

The approach.

Example:

> By combining simple education, guided investing, and transparent pricing.

---

# Vision vs Strategy vs Roadmap

These concepts are often confused.

---

# Product Vision

Vision describes the future state.

It answers:

> "What future are we trying to create?"

Example:

LinkedIn vision:

> Create economic opportunity for every member of the global workforce.

Vision is:

- Long-term
- Inspirational
- Stable

---

# Product Strategy

Strategy explains how to achieve the vision.

It answers:

> "What choices will we make to get there?"

Example:

Focus on:

- Professional networking
- Job discovery
- Career development

Strategy is:

- Directional
- Based on choices
- Flexible

---

# Product Roadmap

Roadmap explains what will be built and when.

Example:

Q1:

Improve onboarding.

Q2:

Launch recommendation engine.

Roadmap is:

- Tactical
- Shorter term
- Changes frequently

---

# Relationship Between Them

```text
Vision
  ↓
Strategy
  ↓
Goals
  ↓
Roadmap
  ↓
Features
```

---

# Example: Stock Trading Platform

## Vision

Make investing accessible to everyone.

---

## Strategy

Focus on beginner investors by simplifying investment decisions.

---

## Goals

- Increase first-time investors
- Improve retention
- Build trust

---

## Roadmap

Features:

- Investment education
- Guided portfolio creation
- Simplified trading interface

---

# Why Product Strategy Matters

## 1. Creates Focus

Without strategy:

Every customer request becomes a priority.

---

With strategy:

Teams know what matters.

---

## 2. Aligns Teams

Different teams understand:

- What we are building
- Why we are building it
- How success is measured

---

## 3. Improves Decision Making

Strategy helps answer:

Should we build this?

Example:

Customer request:

> Add cryptocurrency trading.

Question:

Does this support our strategy?

---

## 4. Creates Competitive Advantage

A clear strategy helps companies differentiate.

---

# Components of Product Strategy

A strong product strategy usually includes:

```text
1. Market Understanding
2. Customer Understanding
3. Product Vision
4. Strategic Goals
5. Differentiation
6. Success Metrics
```

---

# 1. Market Understanding

Before creating strategy, understand the market.

Questions:

- Who are the competitors?
- What trends are changing?
- What opportunities exist?
- What threats exist?

---

# Market Analysis Example: Fintech

Market trends:

- Growth of digital investing
- Increasing mobile adoption
- Demand for financial education

Opportunity:

Help beginner investors enter the market.

---

# 2. Customer Understanding

Strategy must start with customers.

Questions:

- Who are our target users?
- What problems do they have?
- What alternatives do they use?

---

# Customer Segmentation

Not all users are the same.

Example:

Trading platform users:

## Beginners

Need:

- Education
- Confidence
- Guidance

---

## Experienced Investors

Need:

- Advanced tools
- Speed
- Analytics

---

A strategy must decide who to prioritize.

---

# 3. Strategic Goals

Goals translate strategy into measurable outcomes.

Good goals are:

- Specific
- Measurable
- Connected to business impact

---

Example:

Weak:

> Improve user experience.

Strong:

> Increase first-time investment completion from 15% to 30% within six months.

---

# 4. Differentiation

A product needs a reason users choose it.

Question:

> Why should customers choose us instead of competitors?

---

# Types of Differentiation

## Better Experience

Example:

Simpler onboarding.

---

## Lower Cost

Example:

Lower transaction fees.

---

## Unique Capability

Example:

Advanced AI recommendations.

---

## Strong Brand

Example:

Trust and reputation.

---

# Example: Spotify Strategy

Problem:

People want easy access to music.

Possible strategy:

- Personalized discovery
- Massive music library
- Seamless listening experience

Differentiation:

Personalization.

---

# 5. Strategic Bets

A strategy requires making choices.

Companies cannot do everything.

A strategic bet is:

> A major investment based on a belief about the future.

---

Example:

Netflix strategic bet:

Move from DVD rental to streaming.

---

Example:

Trading platform:

Invest heavily in beginner education.

---

# Strategy Requires Saying "No"

A common misconception:

Strategy is about choosing what to do.

Actually:

Strategy is also about choosing what NOT to do.

---

Example:

Company strategy:

"Best platform for beginner investors."

Therefore:

Avoid:

- Advanced institutional trading tools
- Complex derivatives

---

# Product Strategy Framework

A simple framework:

## 1. Target Customer

Who are we serving?

---

## 2. Customer Problem

What important problem are we solving?

---

## 3. Value Proposition

Why are we better?

---

## 4. Strategic Approach

How will we win?

---

## 5. Success Metrics

How will we measure progress?

---

# Example Framework: LinkedIn

## Target Customer

Professionals and companies.

---

## Problem

Professionals need career opportunities and networks.

Companies need talent.

---

## Value Proposition

Professional identity + networking + career opportunities.

---

## Strategy

Become the central platform for professional growth.

---

## Metrics

- Connections created
- Job applications
- Recruiter activity

---

# Product Strategy Mistakes

## Mistake #1

Confusing strategy with a feature list.

Bad strategy:

> Build chat, notifications, AI, analytics.

These are features.

---

Better:

> Help professionals build stronger career networks.

---

## Mistake #2

Trying to serve everyone.

A focused strategy is stronger.

---

## Mistake #3

Ignoring competition.

Markets are competitive.

Understand alternatives.

---

## Mistake #4

No measurable goals.

A strategy without measurement is only a vision.

---

## Mistake #5

Following customer requests blindly.

Customers describe problems.

PMs decide solutions.

---

# Interview Question

## Question

What is the difference between product strategy and roadmap?

## Strong Answer

Product strategy defines the direction, target customers, problems, and approach for achieving business goals. A roadmap translates that strategy into planned initiatives and deliverables. Strategy explains why and where we are going, while the roadmap explains what we will build and when.

# Key Takeaways

1. Strategy defines product direction.
2. Vision explains the future we want to create.
3. Strategy explains how we will achieve it.
4. Roadmaps execute strategy.
5. Great strategies require choices and trade-offs.
6. Customer problems should drive strategy.
7. Product Managers create focus by deciding what matters most.
